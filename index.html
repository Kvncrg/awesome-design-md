Fiscal Year 2025 – 2026

A budget shaped by growth, reappraisal, and a 10-year reinvestment plan.

City Administrator Eric Stuckey presented Franklin's recommended FY26 budget on May 12, 2025. It funds eight new public safety and tech positions, raises water and sewer rates, and restructures the property tax rate after a county-wide reappraisal — all under a framework called Invest Franklin 2.0.

In Plain English

The whole budget, for normal humans.

Skip the jargon. Here's what Franklin's FY 2026 budget actually means for you, your wallet, and your neighborhood.

The city has $243 million to spend this year — that's slightly less than last year, but the main "day-to-day" fund actually grew by almost 10%.

Your property taxes are going up, but mostly because the county re-assessed what your home is worth — Franklin's tax rate still stays one of the lowest in the whole state.

Water and sewer bills are going up in January 2026 — about 6% for water, 5% for sewer.

If you stay at a hotel in Franklin, it costs a tiny bit more — the hotel tax goes from 4% to 5%, and that extra money goes toward parks.

Franklin is hiring more firefighters and police officers — 3 of each, plus a counselor for crime victims.

City workers are getting a raise — at least 2.5%, with a chance to earn up to 5% based on performance.

The biggest construction priority is Mack Hatcher — the city is working with the state to expand it from 2 lanes to 4 lanes in two separate sections.

The city's finances are healthy — low debt, strong savings, and a long-term plan in place.
